Public Transport Experiences

Every tap of an Opal card tells us who is on board. This chart ranks six rider groups by the number of trips they took each year on Sydney’s buses, ferries and Metro. Follow a line to see a group climb or fall.

In 2017, tapping a bank card or phone was the least common way to ride. By 2024 it had overtaken the adult Opal card to become number one.

Each grid has 100 squares, and each square is 1% of all trips on that mode over the latest 12 months. The colours show who made those trips.

Around 4 in 10 trips on both buses and the Metro are now paid with a bank card or phone. Seniors make up about 16% of bus trips, twice their share on the Metro.

Left: an average weekday. Right: an average weekend day. The percentage shows how much of its weekday crowd each mode keeps on weekends.

Trams keep 77% of their weekday riders on weekends, more than any other mode. On weekends, trams carry more people than Melbourne’s trains.

Riders scored eight parts of their trip out of 5, from cost to comfort. The further a line reaches toward the edge, the happier riders are. Rings start at 3, not 0, to make differences visible.

Cost is the highest-rated part of the ride, while frequency of services scores lowest for trains.

Each ring shows the share of services that arrived on time over the past year. A full ring would mean every service ran on time. The grey gap is the share that ran late.

Trams were the most punctual at 96%, followed by trains at 93% and buses at 91%. Note that train figures cover peak hours only.
