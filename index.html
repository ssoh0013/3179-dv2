00 Australia at a glance

One country. Very different ways of moving.

We start with the big picture: which states rely on public transport for the trip to work, and how many people travel between the capitals by rail and coach.

Which states take public transport to work?

  • #NSWTops14Percent
  • #WAOnTopIn2021

Share of workers who travelled to work by public transport only, Census

Each state is coloured by the share of workers who travelled to work by public transport only. Darker blue means a higher share. Use the menu below the map to switch Census years.

Tips to read
  • New South Wales and Victoria sit side by side but are different shades. Compare them first.
  • The ACT is small and easy to miss. Look just inside the south-east corner of New South Wales.
  • Switch Census years and check whether the darkest state stays the same.

01 A day on the network

Every day has a rhythm. So does every network.

Before the disruption, a look at ordinary patterns: how Sydney’s stations fill and empty in the morning, and what happens to Melbourne’s network when school is out.

Where Sydney goes in the morning

  • #Only28of186Gain
  • #CBDTakes58Percent
  • #MostStationsEmptyOut

Morning peak, typical weekday 2023 · circle size = difference between people arriving and leaving at each station

Each circle is a Sydney train station during the morning peak of a typical 2023 weekday. Blue circles gain people (more step off than get on); pink circles lose people. Circle size shows how big that gap is.

Tips to read
  • Circle size shows the gap between arrivals and departures, not how busy a station is overall.
  • Stations with a bold outline are the ones named on the map: dark blue for the biggest gainers, dark pink for the biggest losers.
  • Hover over a circle to fade the others and see its exact arrivals and departures.

When school’s out

  • #EveryModeDips
  • #TramsKeep93
  • #MetroTrainsDrop84

Average weekday trips by mode: school term vs holidays (Melbourne, past year)

Left: an average weekday during school term. Right: an average weekday in the school holidays, with a dashed outline marking the term-time level. The percentage shows how much of its term-time crowd each mode keeps in the holidays.

Tips to read
  • Compare each pink bar with its dashed outline. The empty space is the riders missing in the holidays.
  • Both sides use the same scale, so you can compare bar lengths directly.
  • Regional trains and buses are small. Read their labels rather than the bar lengths.
02 The year the network stopped

In 2020, the trains kept running. The riders didn’t.

COVID-19 emptied the network. Station by station in Sydney and mode by mode in Melbourne, we track how far trips fell and how much has come back.

How COVID emptied the stations

  • #TypicalStation22
  • #AirportsHit2And4
  • #GranvilleHeldUp52

Typical weekday trips (entries + exits) at each train station, as a % of 2019

Each dot is a Sydney train station. Its colour shows weekday trips as a share of the same station’s 2019 level, so darker dots are emptier. Choose a period below the map.

Tips to read
  • Start at Feb 2020, then step through the menu to watch the colours change.
  • Find the airport stations and compare them with stations further west.
  • The notes in the top left update with each period you choose.

The long way back

  • #StillBelow2019
  • #MetroTrains75
  • #RegionalTrains117

Average daily trips by mode in Melbourne, 2019 vs 2025 (% = 2025 compared to 2019 level)

Each row is one way to travel in Melbourne. The pink dot shows average daily trips in 2019 and the blue dot shows 2025. The scale is logarithmic, so gaps of the same length mean the same percentage change.

Tips to read
  • Read each row from pink (2019) to blue (2025). If blue sits on the right, trips have grown.
  • Regional train is the only row where blue sits to the right of pink.
  • Rows are sorted from least to most recovered, so the top rows have the furthest to go.
03 Who came back, and how

Riders came back. Not all at once, and not in the same way.

The comeback has not been even. Cities recovered at different speeds, and today’s riders differ in who they are and how they pay.

The recovery race

  • #PerthOnlyAbove
  • #MelbourneLast5of6
  • #HobartFirstToSeventh

Capital cities ranked by public transport trips as a share of 2018-19 (pre-COVID)

Each line is a capital city. Every financial year, the cities are ranked by public transport trips as a share of their own 2018-19 level, so first place is the city closest to its pre-COVID level.

Tips to read
  • Where two lines cross, one city has overtaken another.
  • Dashed circles mark the moments described in the notes on the chart.
  • Rank shows order, not size: a city can rank first and still be below its 2018-19 level. Hover over a point to see the exact share.

Who rides what in Sydney

  • #Seniors3in10OnBuses
  • #MetroMostlyAdults
  • #SeniorsHalfOnMetro

Each square = 1% of Adult, Child/Student and Senior trips on that mode, latest 12 months

Each grid has 100 squares, one grid per mode. Each square is 1% of adult, child or student, and senior trips on that mode over the latest 12 months, coloured by rider group.

Tips to read
  • Each row of 10 squares is 10%, so you can count rows instead of single squares.
  • Compare the same colour across the three grids to see which mode each group uses most.
  • Only adult, child/student and senior trips are counted. Other ticket types are left out.

Tap and go in Sydney

  • #Tap42OnBuses
  • #Tap42OnMetro
  • #FerriesTap57

Monthly trips by payment type (Bus · Ferry · Metro), stacked around the centre · each panel has its own scale

Each band shows monthly trips on one mode, split by payment: pink for contactless (tap) and blue for Opal and other tickets. Each panel has its own scale, so compare shapes rather than heights.

Tips to read
  • Watch the pink band after each grey dashed line, which marks when tapping started on that mode.

  • Each panel has its own scale. Compare how much of each band is pink, not how thick the panels are.
04 The ride itself

Getting there is one thing. Enjoying the ride is another.

Trip counts only tell part of the story. Here Queensland riders rate their journeys, and we check how often services actually run on time.

Rating the ride in Queensland

  • #CostRatedHighest
  • #TrainFrequencyLowest

Average rider satisfaction by mode, Queensland, Jan–Jun 2026 (rings from 3 to 5 out of 5)

Each coloured line is one mode. Riders scored eight parts of their trip out of 5, and points further from the centre mean higher scores.

Tips to read
  • On each spoke, the outermost point is the mode riders rated best for that aspect.
  • Follow the purple Ferry line all the way round and compare it with the other modes.
  • Rings start at 3, not 0, so gaps look bigger than they are.

On time in Queensland, 2025–26

  • #TramsOnTime96
  • #1in11BusesLate

Share of services running on time, Jul 2025 – Jun 2026 (full arc = 100%) · Train: peak hours only · Bus: all day · Tram: all day

Each ring is one mode. The coloured arc shows the share of services that ran on time, where a full arc would be 100%. The grey gap is the share that ran late.

Tips to read
  • Check where each arc ends against the 90%, 95% and 100% lines on the left.
  • All three arcs are close to full, so the differences are only a few percentage points.
  • The “1 in X late” labels turn the grey gap into an everyday number.
